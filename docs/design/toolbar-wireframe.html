Week 6: How cities plan for water

Cities depend on reliable water supply, but planning for it involves many groups with different goals. This reading introduces the main ideas you will use in this week's assignment.

First, planners estimate demand. They look at how many people live in an area, how that number is changing, and how much water homes, schools and businesses use each day.

Next, they compare demand with supply. Supply can come from rivers, reservoirs, groundwater or recycled water, and each source has costs and risks.

Your notes


Finally, planners decide how to close the gap, for example by reducing leaks, changing prices or building new storage. Section 2 looks at each option in turn.
